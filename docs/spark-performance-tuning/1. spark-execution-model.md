## Spark Execution Model

Before you tune a single configuration, you must deeply internalize how Spark actually executes your code. This is the single most important prerequisite to everything else in this guide.

When you write a DataFrame transformation in Spark, nothing happens immediately. Spark builds a logical plan — a tree of operations — and waits. This is lazy evaluation, and it’s both a blessing and a source of endless confusion for beginners.

The moment you call an action (.count(), .show(), .write()), Spark kicks off the Catalyst optimizer, which takes your logical plan through several stages:

Logical Plan → Analyzed Logical Plan → Optimized Logical Plan → Physical Plan → Executed Plan

The Catalyst optimizer applies hundreds of rules to rewrite your query. It pushes filters as early as possible, eliminates redundant projections, and chooses join strategies. Understanding this pipeline is why reading query plans (our next section) is so critical — it shows you what Spark actually decided to do with your code, not what you think you told it to do.

At runtime, Spark breaks your job into Jobs → Stages → Tasks:

- A Job is triggered by a single action.
- Stages are groups of tasks that can be executed in parallel without shuffling data across the network. A stage boundary is always a shuffle.
- Tasks are the atomic units of work, one per partition. Each task processes a single partition of data on a single executor core.

This model has a profound implication: if you reduce the number of stages (by reducing shuffles), you dramatically reduce your job’s latency. This is the mental model you should carry into every optimization decision.
